Native

Written in Swift with AppKit. No web view, no Electron, no background polling. It uses no CPU until a finger lands on the edge.

Readable

Rimshot needs Accessibility permission to press keys for you, so you should be able to see exactly what it does. The whole app is one file of Swift, right here.

Every MacBook

Any MacBook with a Force Touch trackpad and macOS 13 or later, including the Airs that never had a Touch Bar. MIT licensed.

Install in a minute. macOS asks you to approve it once.

01

Download Rimshot.dmg, open it, and drag Rimshot into Applications.

02

Open it. macOS says it could not verify the app, because it isn't notarized yet. Click Done.

03

In System Settings → Privacy & Security, click Open Anyway next to the Rimshot message.

04

Turn on Accessibility when Rimshot asks, then open it again. It lives in your menu bar.

Prefer Terminal? This replaces step 3: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Rimshot.app

Or build it yourself and skip the warning entirely.

Questions

Why does it need Accessibility?

To press keys like volume and escape for you, and to stop a tap on the bar from also clicking whatever is under your cursor. It doesn't read what you type.

Can I pick my own buttons?

Not yet. Custom buttons and per-app sets are next. If you have opinions, open an issue.

Could a macOS update break it?

Possibly. Reading finger positions takes a private Apple framework, since there's no public way to do it. If something stops working, please report it.

Why isn't it notarized?

Notarizing takes a paid Apple developer account. It's on the list. Until then, the Open Anyway step is a one-time thing.
